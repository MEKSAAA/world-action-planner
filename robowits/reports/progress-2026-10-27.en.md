The native-interface audit, five complete labeled-subgoal baselines, contact replay, reward controls, candidate-diversity check and split audit are complete. The three-condition experiment on new layouts is in progress. **There is no completed evidence of a task-success improvement from WM selection yet.** No π0.5 training was run; Amber's training code, configuration, data and checkpoint remain unchanged.

## Reward and candidate audits

Two previous WM windows were tested in original/reversed order, with identical candidates and with static frames. One genuine transport window supplied a separate positive control; ground-truth future frames never enter action selection.

- **Qwen3.5-9B:** high scores and invented grasp progress on static clips; joint scoring also exhibited order sensitivity. Independent scoring did not fix the visual error.
- **Cosmos-Reason2-8B:** copied a filled schema example; after removing the example, it also rejected genuine transport progress. Failed the positive control.
- **Claude, independent scoring:** static and identical controls scored zero; nearly static predictions varied by up to 0.05. A genuine 0.3-second transport clip scored 0.10 versus 0.05 for static; a 2-second clip scored 0.70 versus 0.05. These few controls are encouraging, not comprehensive calibration or human approval.

The pilot freezes `claude-fable-5-1`, independent candidate scoring and a required improvement greater than 0.10 over valid candidate zero. This margin exceeds observed control variation but is not a statistically calibrated guarantee. All-invalid judgments stop evaluation explicitly and are not counted as task failures.

Eight action samples at three states in episode 105 yielded mean pairwise intended endpoint translation differences of approximately **2.0, 3.1 and 7.9 mm**. Repeated candidate zero was identical within the same worker. These are command-space differences, not physical rollout distances or evidence of distinct strategies.

The VLA predicts only ten steps, approximately 0.333 seconds. The WM requires at least one second; the robot proxy holds the final EE target to fill the tail. The judge sees only the first six WM frames, or 0.30 seconds. Padding can still influence WM generation. The genuine-video controls show why this short horizon may be insufficient for action ranking.

## Split audit and paired experiment

WM training uses episodes 105–149; policy validation uses 105, 120, 130, 140 and 149. The WM holdout, 100–102, belongs to policy training according to its configuration. There is **no joint holdout among these fifty episodes**.

Three fixed new layouts translate all task objects in seed scenes 10–12 by predeclared offsets. Their nearest recorded initial layouts have mean object-origin distances of approximately 6.5–7.4 cm. These are small layout changes within the same task, not new-task generalization.

Each trial runs up to 1,000 steps, with two candidates and five executed actions per decision:

1. Policy baseline: execute candidate zero.
2. Forced-zero control: run WM and VLM, but execute candidate zero.
3. Selection: execute the candidate selected by the frozen scoring rule.

Before the first alternative is executed, subgoals are reused from the baseline and observations and executed trajectories must match. Separate policy processes produced small numerical output differences despite identical inputs and seeds. Therefore, after exact checks of every policy input, prompt and seed, the paired run uses the same saved baseline draw for candidate zero. Fresh samples and their discrepancies are retained. Selection also reuses the control's sampled candidate set at identical states.

WM predictions and judgments may be reused only with identical inputs and verified provenance. Saved compute time is recorded separately: cached wall time must not be presented as deployment inference cost. This is a three-layout feasibility pilot, not a statistically powered benchmark. Full paired results remain pending.

All three autonomous VLM-subgoal policy baselines have now completed 1,000 steps each: **0/3 successes**. Contact replays have zero joint error and no finger–lemon contact above 0.01 N. Maximum lifts are 12.9, 0.04 and 9.0 mm, all below the 25 mm threshold. The corrected forced-zero run has completed its first five actions with exact baseline agreement; both candidates scored 0.35. At roughly two minutes per decision, the full WM comparison is estimated to take 20–40 hours depending on divergence and reusable predictions.
