## 1. WAP: what was actually tested

We used the released WAP world model and checkpoints to test whether future video is predicted correctly given robot motion conditions. This is the part directly comparable to the KinDER report.

| Component | Use in this experiment | Question addressed |
|---|---|---|
| WAP world model | Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B backbone; released weights evaluated zero-shot and finetuned on lemon | Are robot motion, scene appearance, and object consequences predicted correctly? |
| Existing RoboWits policies | Run existing π0.5 / ACT checkpoints in the native environment | Can the policy complete the lemon task? |
| World-model action selection | New minimal loop: two candidate action sequences → WM predictions → image scoring → execution | Does selection change actions and improve actual task outcomes? |
| Full planner from the WAP paper | Not reproduced here | These results are not the success rate of the paper's complete WAP system |

**Video prediction metrics and task success are reported separately.** Rendering the robot correctly does not establish that a policy can grasp the lemon. Apparent success in generated video does not establish success in the simulator.

## 2. Zero-shot evaluation of the two released world models

### Setup

- Existing lemon data only: source episodes 100–149, 50 episodes in total.
- Held-out test: episodes 100–102, three episodes with four windows each, for **12 windows**.
- Source video is 30 Hz and is resampled to 20 Hz. Each test window contains **80 history frames + 40 future frames**, covering two seconds of future motion.
- Three actual views: ego, right wrist, and left wrist. The WAP four-panel input uses `ego | ego / wrist_right | wrist_left`. The two upper panels duplicate the same view; **these are not four independent cameras**.
- Offline evaluation constructs skeleton conditions from recorded future robot states. It tests video prediction given future motion, not autonomous action generation.
- **Copy-last:** repeat the last history image throughout the future.
- **Frozen condition:** replace all future skeletons with the last history skeleton to test whether predictions depend on robot motion conditions.

### Results: PSNR (dB; higher is better)

| View | libero_90_base | robosuite_ft | Copy-last |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ego | 16.30 | 16.06 | **16.88** |
| Right wrist | 12.72 | 11.82 | **13.28** |
| Left wrist | 16.75 | 16.65 | **17.63** |

Neither released model exceeds copy-last in mean PSNR for any of the three views. For `robosuite_ft`, ego robot-region MSE is 5,826 with the true condition versus 6,717 with the frozen condition, a ratio of only about 1.15. Wrist views do not show a consistent benefit from the true condition.

**Takeaway:** zero-shot predictions show some response to motion, but transfer across robots and scenes is insufficient to establish reliable prediction of lemon interactions.

### Contact and object behavior: qualitative review

All 12 held-out windows were reviewed at eight sampled future times per window, with six additional detailed multi-view images. Per-frame object masks have not been annotated, so we do not report object-region MSE or contact-event pass rates comparable to those in the KinDER report.

| Event | Observation after finetuning | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Arm motion and scene appearance | Substantially closer to ground-truth video than zero-shot predictions | Clear improvement |
| Grasping and moving grey obstacles | Some displacement trends are captured, but pose and timing errors remain | Partial improvement |
| Approaching the lemon and closing the gripper | The lemon is retained in some windows and omitted in others | Not reliably resolved |
| Carrying a grasped object | In one window, the wrist view retains the grasped lemon while the ego view generates an extra yellow object | Object count and cross-view consistency remain problematic |
| Releasing and placing | Some predictions omit the lemon on the target; others release it at the wrong time | Not reliably resolved |

**Takeaways:** video prediction and skeleton following improve on held-out trajectories of the same task. Reliable grasping, carrying, and placement prediction have not been established. Generalization to new tasks or layouts, and the correctness of collision or penetration behavior, remain untested.

## 4. Actual task execution: existing policy baselines

The task requires manipulating the lemon in a scene with obstacles and placing it in the target region. Success uses the native Genesis131 `is_success` criterion. **A progress score of 0.5 does not mean a 50% success rate.**

| Policy | Evaluation | Successes | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Existing π0.5 | Fixed scenes 10–14; up to 1,000 steps per episode | **0/5** | Paired baseline for the WM experiment |
| Existing ACT | Separate development scenes 0–4; up to 1,000 steps per episode | **0/5** | Different scenes; not a strictly paired comparison between policies |

All five π0.5 trajectories were replayed to inspect native contact forces. Recorded joint trajectories were reproduced step by step with a maximum error of zero:

- In 2/5 episodes, no effective fingertip–lemon contact was recorded.
- In 3/5 episodes, fingertips contacted the lemon but did not lift it effectively.
- Maximum lemon lift across episodes was only about 0.11–5.94 mm. Failures occurred before or during grasp establishment; there is no evidence supporting a “dropped during transport” diagnosis for these episodes.
- The contact-force threshold was 0.01 N. Contact with obstacle panels can be intentional and is not automatically a collision failure.

### Status of π0.5 single-task training

Finetuning the existing π0.5 action expert on lemon was started, then **stopped at the user's request, together with automatic follow-up evaluation**. A 500-step intermediate snapshot scored 0/3 on development scenes 0–2; this is not a final-model result.

Training used the existing LeRobot entry point inside Genesis131 without modifying its original training code. New data adaptation code lives in the WAP repository. An early conversion incorrectly used world coordinates; it was corrected to robot-base coordinates and validated, and the affected trial runs were marked invalid. This policy training is separate from the completed 2k-step WAP world-model finetuning.

## 5. Policy + world model: closed-loop candidate selection

### Setup

- Uses the **original π0.5 weights**, not weights from the stopped training run.
- Both conditions use scenes 10–14. Initial states were checked to match for each pair.
- The policy replans every 30 native control steps and executes 30 steps at a time.
- At steps 90, 180, …, 900, two sampled policy candidates are compared. Selection stops when an episode terminates early.
- A separate robot-only scene, containing no task objects, converts candidate actions into future skeletons. The WM predicts 20 frames at 20 Hz, covering one second.
- Scoring uses only the distance between the yellow lemon and the green target in the final five predicted ego frames. Candidate 1 is selected only if both candidates can be scored and its improvement exceeds 0.02 of the image width.
- If the lemon is missing or duplicated, or the target is not visible, selection falls back to the original candidate. True future object states, rewards, and RGB observations are not used for scoring.

### Completed runs: raw records, not evidence of a causal benefit

| Scene | Original policy | Run with WM | WM selection rounds | Alternative selections | Original / with-WM time (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | Failure | Failure | 10 | 0 | 57.5 / 1049.8 |
| 11 | Failure | Failure | 10 | 0 | 48.1 / 2182.3 |
| 12 | Failure | **Success, 670 steps** | 7 | **0** | 46.9 / 932.4 |
| 13 | Failure | Failure | 10 | 0 | 48.5 / 1308.6 |
| 14 | Failure | Failure | 10 | 2 | 44.9 / 1400.9 |
| Total / mean | **0/5** | **1/5** | **47** | **2** | **49.2 / 1374.8** |

Mean additional time is approximately **1,325.6 seconds (22.1 minutes) per episode**. The machine had concurrent workloads, and GPU assignment changed during the experiment. These are observed wall-clock times, not dedicated-GPU performance measurements.

### Attribution issue that must be resolved first

**Scene 12, the only successful run, never selected an alternative action. Its success cannot be attributed to the WM choosing a better candidate.**

A fresh check of `trajectory.npz` during preparation of the original report found identical action and joint trajectories for scene 10. However, scenes 11, 12, and 13 had different action trajectories between conditions despite zero alternative selections. For scene 12, the maximum absolute difference in the 14-dimensional action arrays over their shared time prefix is approximately 0.872. These arrays mix position, rotation, and gripper values, so this number must not be interpreted as meters. Matching initial states alone does not guarantee a consistent closed-loop control comparison.

The source of this divergence remains unresolved. Candidate-0 randomness, numerical determinism during inference, and resumed execution paths need inspection. We can currently report that “one run with the WM achieved native task success,” but **not that “the WM improved success from 0% to 20%.”** Scene 14, where selection actually changed, still failed. A benefit from candidate selection has not been demonstrated.

## Next steps

1. **Resolve closed-loop control consistency first.** Add a control that runs the WM but always selects candidate 0. Compare observations, candidate actions, and executed trajectories step by step against the original policy to explain scene 12's success without alternative selection.
2. **Check the existing policy's configuration and capability.** Verify the control interface, preprocessing, and execution frequency expected by its weights to distinguish policy limitations from configuration mismatches. Do not resume π0.5 training.
3. **Add object and contact-event evaluation.** Annotate the lemon in grasping, carrying, and release windows; assess disappearance, duplication, position, and cross-view consistency instead of relying only on whole-image PSNR.
4. **Evaluate WM benefits after the controls are reliable.** A one-second lemon-to-target distance score poorly captures the long-term value of moving obstacles before grasping. Define a more suitable score and independent test scenes, then compare task success, actual alternative selections, and added latency.
